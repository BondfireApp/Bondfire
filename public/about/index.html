ABOUT BONDFIRE

Private infrastructure for people doing things together.

Bondfire is a modular coordination platform for mutual-aid networks, community organizations, collectives, gatherings, projects, and other groups that need useful infrastructure without turning themselves into a data product.

Get started How zero-knowledge works

WHY IT EXISTS

Useful software should not require surrendering your organization's private life.

Most online organization tools ask groups to centralize a great deal of information: conversations, membership records, documents, schedules, contact information, internal decisions, and organizational history.

Bondfire takes a different approach. Private organization content is encrypted on the client before it is persisted. The server stores encrypted data rather than needing access to the underlying private content, and authorized members decrypt the information they have permission to use.

MODULAR BY DESIGN

Use what the group needs. Leave the rest off.

Bondfire centers each group in an Organization Workspace. Groups can enable tools for needs, pledges, inventory, meetings, files, events, capture, communication, intake, collaborative work, publishing, and other coordination tasks without adopting one enormous all-or-nothing platform.

PRIVATE

Private by default

Private organization content is encrypted rather than treated as platform-readable data.

MODULAR

Tools fit the work

Organizations enable the parts of Bondfire that are actually useful to them.

PORTABLE

Hosted or self-hosted

Bondfire can be used through the hosted service or deployed independently.

PUBLIC WHEN CHOSEN

Publishing stays explicit

Organization Pages and Publication Sites are intentional public surfaces, separate from private workspace data.

SECURITY WITHOUT THE FAIRY TALE

Zero-knowledge is a boundary, not a magic spell.

Bondfire is designed so the infrastructure storing private organization content does not need a readable copy of that content. That does not mean the service knows literally nothing about accounts, requests, devices, or the fact that activity occurred.

Public means public.

Information a group deliberately publishes through an Organization Page, Publication Site, public event, or other public surface is intentionally readable. Encryption also does not replace good device security, membership practices, backups, or careful decisions about what information a group collects in the first place.

THE IDEA

Groups should be able to coordinate without creating another institution that has to be trusted with everything.

Software will not create strong communities. It can at least try not to make their work harder, collect more than it needs, or quietly turn coordination into somebody else's dataset.
